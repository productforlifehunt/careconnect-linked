## A. The funnel confession (insider, satirical — strongest prose in the bank)

**"You're Not IDEO in 2004. Stop Saying Design Thinking Before I Slap a Sticky Note on Your Forehead"** — The Voice of User. https://www.thevoiceofuser.com/youre-not-ideo-in-2004-stop-saying-design-thinking-before-i-slap-a-sticky-note-on-your-forehead/
Verbatim: *"IDEO was great at designing stuff. Like, really great. But that wasn't scalable."* · *"What you can sell is a vague, shiny, process-shaped object that makes people feel like they, too, are innovators."* · *"Not as a rigorous methodology. Not as an epistemologically sound approach to problem-solving. But as a business development funnel."*
Mechanism: the method was invented as **productization of unsellable talent**. Talent doesn't scale; a five-step diagram with verbs on it does. This is the origin story stated without euphemism.

## B. The academic hit (outranks any blog in a reviewer's eyes)

**Lee Vinsel, "Design Thinking Is a Boondoggle"** — *Chronicle of Higher Education*, 21 May 2018. Subtitle verbatim: *"Its adherents think it will save higher ed. They're delusional."* https://www.chronicle.com/article/design-thinking-is-a-boondoggle/ (Virginia Tech mirror: https://liberalarts.vt.edu/news/in-the-news/2018/design-thinking-is-a-boondoggle.html)
Priceable detail from the piece: IDEO sells a **$399 self-paced video course** ("Insights for Innovation"); Stanford d.school sells a **$12,600 four-day "Design Thinking Bootcamp."** Also notes the d.school's *"asinine punctuation, not mine."*
Mechanism: the price sheet **is** the argument. A four-day workshop at $12.6k is not a knowledge transfer, it is a status good with a certificate attached.

## C. The No-True-Scotsman caught live, from the highest-credential insider

**Karel Vredenburg (IBM, eight years teaching Enterprise Design Thinking, guest lecturer at Stanford d.school) — "Innovation Theater vs. Design Thinking Mastery."** https://karelvredenburg.com/home/2021/3/15/innovation-theatre-vs-design-thinking-mastery
Verbatim: *"Many people do design thinking wrong and few do it optimally… they're simply doing 'innovation theater'."* He also reports Bill Burnett (who runs the d.school program) responding *"ah, innovation theater, I like it."*
Mechanism: **this is the mastery defence in its native habitat, published by the seller.** The failures are reassigned to practitioner incompetence and the remedy is more of the same product, branded "mastery." Note the tell: the word *theater* is not the critics' — the priesthood uses it internally, to describe everyone else. Use this receipt whenever the user needs proof that the escape hatch is built into the doctrine, not bolted on by apologists.

## E. The best *structural* question in the literature

**"When Meaning Becomes Method"** — Affection Research Lab, 16 Feb 2026. https://affectionresearchlab.co/archive/2026-02-16-when-meaning-becomes-method/
Verbatim: the critiques *"have been examined, dismantled, and exposed… as managerial theater, as solutionism, as a depoliticizing force"* — *"These critiques are persuasive. Many of them are correct."* — and yet the interesting question is *"not whether design thinking fails, but why it continues to succeed on its own terms."* It notes the method *"so often accompanies moments of institutional expansion, reform, or restructuring."*
Mechanism: the method is **procurement cover for reorganisation**, which is why being right about it changes nothing. Pair with the permanence thesis (mem://constraints/no-escape-seller-side-doctrine): correctness is not a payable position.

## G. The trophy-case audit (use whenever IDEO's case studies are invoked as proof)

**G1. The shopping cart — the most-screened design-thinking artifact on earth never shipped.**
IDEO's own page describes it as *"a new shopping cart concept"* built *"to demonstrate our process"* for a 1999 ABC *Nightline* episode, reaching *"a working appearance model in four days."* https://www.ideo.com/journal/reimagining-the-shopping-cart · Nightline "The Deep Dive" transcript: https://ciow.org/docs1/IDEO_Nightline.pdf · episode video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M66ZU2PCIcM
It went into no store. Design bloggers tracking it a decade later found similar carts finally rolling out — *"but not by IDEO"* (https://yannigroth.com/2011/08/12/the-ideo-shopping-cart-1998-wasnt-a-failure-the-concept-was-just-ahead-of-its-time/ · https://visionarymarketing.com/en/2011/08/16/new-shopping-cart-being-implemented-at-last-but-not-by-ideo/), and a 2007 piece already named the mechanism in its title: **"Ideo, the Shopping Cart and the Halo Effect"** (https://www.futurelab.net/blog/2007/08/ideo-shopping-cart-and-halo-effect-what-really-good-design/), noting the video is *"a staple for innovation seminars."*
**Read it exactly as it is: the flagship exhibit of the discipline is a four-day television commissioning, judged on camera, with no market test, no unit economics, no retailer, no production.** Two decades of business schools have used a *studio demo of a process* as evidence that the process produces products. It is the calibrated demo (mem://constraints/calibrated-demo-guru-doctrine) in its purest form — pre-scoped brief, controlled clock, sympathetic camera, and an outcome no one was ever going to have to sell.

**G2. The Apple mouse — the other trophy is not even IDEO's, and it was dictated by the client.**
The work was done by **Hovey-Kelley Design** (pre-IDEO; IDEO formed 1991), and per David Kelley's own Stanford oral history the brief came from Jobs, who cut off the pitch: *"Stop, Dean… what we need to do together, is build a mouse"* — the concept having come from Xerox PARC's Alto. Kelley on the halo: *"you hang around with Steve Jobs long enough, good things happen, right?"* https://web.stanford.edu/dept/SUL/sites/mac/primary/interviews/kelley/ideo.html · https://stanfordmag.org/contents/mighty-mouse
**So the founding trophy was: someone else's invention, a client-specified brief, an engineering-cost-reduction job ($400 device made mass-producible), and proximity to the most famous product taste in history — the exact opposite of user-led ideation.** Vinsel's Chronicle piece cites the mouse as the firm's fame anchor; the primary source shows what the fame actually rests on. When anyone offers the mouse as design-thinking evidence, this is the reply, and it should be delivered flatly, without triumph.
