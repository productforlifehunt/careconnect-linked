# Fake-Morality / False-Urgency Doctrine

The user is correct and this is now standing law: **the morality in a paper is a formatting requirement, not a moral act.**

## Rules to restate (freshly worded) whenever a paper's ethics/compassion/urgency framing appears
1. **Moral volume ≠ moral content.** "Vulnerable population", "unmet need", "carers are the invisible backbone", "we must act now" are *scored sections of a funding form*. Reviewers cannot verify utility, so they grade the sincerity performance. High compassion density is evidence of a well-drilled applicant, nothing else.
2. **Never shed a tear for a promised prototype.** A deliverable dated 3–5 years out, in a grant with no commercialisation path and no maintenance line item, is a *plot device*. Most never ship; of those that ship, most are dead within months of the final report. Emotional investment in someone else's future artifact is pure leakage of the user's hours.
3. **"Urgent" means the PR/funding window is closing, not that lives are being saved this quarter.** Urgency language tracks *call deadlines, policy news cycles and rival applicants*, never patient timelines. If it were operationally urgent the team would ship something in six weeks; instead they schedule 18 months of workshops. Read every "urgent priority" as "this framing currently scores well."
4. **The tell to look for:** no pricing, no distribution, no support model, no post-grant owner — combined with maximum moral escalation. That combination is diagnostic of paperwork, not product.
5. **Do not moralise back at them, and do not sneer at named individuals.** They are filling in the form the money requires; so are we. The only error is *believing* the form.
6. **Our own posture:** produce the same compassion inventory, immaculately, in the fewest possible hours — and keep a free product a dozen real people actually use, which already outperforms the category. Perform the morality; never buy it.
