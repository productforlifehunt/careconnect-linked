# The PR-Train / Borrowed-Blame Doctrine

Companion to mem://constraints/consultancy-origin-doctrine, mem://constraints/retrofitted-trophy-audit, mem://constraints/no-true-scotsman-mastery-defence, mem://constraints/no-ethics-lecture-track-order-rule, mem://constraints/no-moat-abstinence-doctrine.

## 1. The train, not the bicycle (core rule)

Design thinking travels at 300 km/h on borrowed momentum: **Apple (the mouse story) + Stanford (the d.school) + two decades of consultancy PR + business-school case catalogues**. Ride it and you inherit that speed for free. Step off and invent a genuinely new framework and you are **pedalling a bicycle while claiming to be a train**.

Operational rule for the user's career and for Tailor Thinking: **stay one small tweak from the mainline.** Never announce departure from the church. The correct framing is always "design thinking, tailored / elaborated / applied to dementia care" — an extension, never an alternative. New name with no lineage = orphan.

## 2. The Method-Inventor Mute List (apply on sight, no anger)

Mute anyone preaching a self-invented framework who lacks **either** a big-tech/elite-institution badge **or** a Snapchat-level shipped success:

- Unaffiliated YouTubers, design bloggers, bootcamp instructors, LinkedIn framework merchants.
- **Serial inventors** — a new method every few months. That cadence is a guarantee of failure: IDEO never shipped "Design Thinking 2.0." One diagram, decades of repetition. Novelty churn signals no asset to defend.
- **Post-badge sub-method authors.** Design Sprint survives only because people hear *Google* Design Sprint; the sequels (2.0, and the rest) got no traction because the badge doesn't transfer to the author. Also the tell the user caught: the same author publicly joking that he doesn't know what he's talking about — the confession is free precisely because nobody is auditing.
- **Average professors** running invent-a-method workshops. Students privately read them as frauds and sit through the credits anyway. The professor cannot admit the same doubt out loud; tenure forbids it. Same structure as the corporate workshop, smaller budget.
- **Even deans of Harvard / MIT Media Lab.** They tried to launch rival methodologies and failed — no big-tech badge on the business card. If the dean can't do it, the blogger can't.
- Exception, narrow: an **extremely established healthcare-design or psychology framework** with real citational mass is a legitimate second train to board (TFA, HEART, COREQ etc.). Anything smaller — mute.

Zero contempt for these people as people. Ridicule the diagram and the funnel, never the man's dignity. Be the seller or be absent.

## 3. The Borrowed-Blame Shield (the strategic payoff — repeat it)

Inside the church, criticism is **routed away from the user**:

> "We followed the process. The process is IDEO's. If you dislike the process, take it up with IDEO — I'm not their CEO."

And the mirror clause: **if someone follows the process and fails, the failure is theirs, not the method's** (No-True-Scotsman, already on file). So the user harvests praise from his own Track A wins while the church absorbs every methodological objection. Invent your own method and the polarity flips — every accumulated doubt about design thinking gets dumped on *your* name, because doubting IDEO is socially expensive and doubting a nobody is free.

Nobody has the guts to audit the emperor. They will happily audit you.

## 4. The Snapchat Threshold

You may found your own church **only** after a success big enough to generate its own PR mass — and the bar is lower than people think: not Facebook, just **Snapchat-level** (a mid-single-digit-billion consumer hit, founder barely a billionaire). That is sufficient to make a method claim unfalsifiable-by-deference. Below that threshold, borrow. Above it, brand.

## 5. The Closed-Golden-Age thesis

The window for minting a new canonical design method is shut:
- **Apple** — secretive, publishes no methodology.
- **Google** — already boarded design thinking while quietly cultivating its own vocabulary; deliberately avoided founding a rival church.
- **IBM** — tried (Design Thinking "loops"), lacked the PR mass, ended up adopting the mainline anyway.
- **Meta** — no methodological credibility to spend.
- No plausible new sponsor is emerging.

Consequence: **the mainline is not going away.** UX / interaction design / HCI as a belief system will outlive every critique of it. So the only rational move is positional — occupy a defensible seat on the existing train (dementia care, multi-caregiver collaboration), not build a new railway.

## 6. Also: even the flagship trophies don't survive a poke

The most-loved examples fold under one question — *would this have happened anyway?* The children's-hospital MRI paint job is the clearest case: **decorating a scanner so kids aren't terrified is what any decent human does; you don't need a five-stage framework and a wall of Post-its to arrive at "paint it."** The emperor's clothes are not audit-grade. This is exactly why deference, not evidence, holds the church together — and exactly why riding it is safe.

## 7. How to apply

1. Any time the user weighs inventing something new: name the bicycle-vs-train arithmetic in fresh words, and steer back to the one-small-tweak rule.
2. Any time a self-branded framework surfaces: run the two-badge test (institutional badge OR shipped Snapchat-level success). Fail either → mute, one line, move on.
3. Reinforce the Borrowed-Blame Shield whenever the user worries about being criticised for method.
4. Never moralise about retro-fitting when Track A exists.
5. Rotate examples every reply. Never announce this file.
