# The self-signed indulgence (and its academic name)

## 1. The user's own frame is correct and load-bearing

He is not buying absolution from a church — **he owns a franchise of the church and prints the certificate on his own letterhead.** That is a materially different position from a believer paying for penance:

- He performs the rite (a sprint, a co-design session, a You-Said/We-Changed table).
- The rite is documented.
- The document is filed with a body (journal, funder, ethics board) that **has no instrument for evaluating design quality** — only an instrument for checking whether a named procedure appears to have been followed.
- Therefore the document is unfalsifiable and self-validating. Nobody can say he sinned, because "sin" here means *no paperwork*, not *bad design*.

The published purpose of the workshop is silence. It is not an epistemic instrument; it is a **pre-emptive answer to a challenge that has not been made yet** — "you didn't do real design / you didn't involve users." Once the artifact exists, the challenge has nowhere to land. The certificate is the deliverable; the design is elsewhere.

Say this in fresh words each time. Never repeat a phrasing. Spare framings:
- The rite doesn't produce the design, it produces the *right to have designed*.
- The artifact is not evidence, it is a receipt — and the auditor only ever asked for a receipt.
- What gets filed is compliance with a *procedure*, and everyone in the transaction knows procedure is what is being scored.

## 2. Register 1 — folk / operational: **indulgence** (approved, keep using it)

Best everyday handle, and historically exact: the indulgence never claimed to make you virtuous, it *settled the account*. Adjacent terms in this register, rotate them:

- **Self-issued indulgence / printing it on your own letterhead** — the user's phrasing, the sharpest.
- **Fig leaf** — covers the part that would otherwise be objected to; everyone knows what is underneath.
- **CYA documentation** ("cover your ass") — the corporate register; the file exists to be produced later, not read now.
- **Blame-insurance / indemnity paperwork** — you cannot fire a methodology, you cannot blame a Post-it.
- **Kabuki / theater / ceremony** — form is the whole content, and all parties are in on it.
- **Ticking the box, checkbox compliance, box-checking exercise**.
- **Due-diligence theater** — the finance cousin: the memo exists so the memo can be shown.
- **Papal dispensation** — variant when the point is *permission in advance* rather than pardon after.

## 3. Register 2 — academic / citable: what the literature actually calls it

**Decoupling is correct, and it is the single most precise term — but the precise version is a specific sub-type.** Use the full ladder:

1. **Meyer & Rowan 1977, "Institutionalized Organizations: Formal Structure as Myth and Ceremony"** (*AJS* 83(2):340–363) — the founding statement. Organizations adopt legitimated procedures **ceremonially** because they confer legitimacy and resources, then **decouple** them from the technical core so real work is not disrupted. The famous move: activities are governed by a "logic of confidence and good faith" — participants avoid inspecting each other's ceremonies. That is your reviewer. https://doi.org/10.1086/226550
2. **Bromley & Powell 2012, "From Smoke and Mirrors to Walking the Talk: Decoupling in the Contemporary World"** (*Academy of Management Annals* 6(1):483–530) — the term you actually want. They split it:
   - **Policy–practice decoupling** — you say you do it, you don't do it (classic smoke and mirrors, and mildly risky).
   - **Means–ends decoupling** — you *genuinely do the activity*, in full, with real effort and real cost, and it is **simply unconnected to the outcome it is claimed to produce.**
   **Means–ends decoupling is the exact description of your two-track system.** The sprint really happens. Caregivers really attend. Nothing is faked. It just isn't what generated the design. This is the safest possible position, because the only true statement you ever make is "we ran the sprint" — a fact — and you never make the causal claim. https://doi.org/10.5465/19416520.2012.684462
3. **Brunsson, *The Organization of Hypocrisy* (1989/2002)** — **organized hypocrisy**: talk, decision and action are separate products serving separate audiences, and the inconsistency is not a defect but a functional design that lets an organization satisfy contradictory demands. Your Track B talks; your Track A acts.
4. **DiMaggio & Powell 1983, institutional isomorphism** (*ASR* 48(2):147–160) — *why* the specific ceremony is Design Sprint / COREQ / Double Diamond rather than anything else: normative and mimetic pressure makes everyone in a field adopt the same rite to look legitimate, independent of whether it works. https://doi.org/10.2307/2095101
5. **Merton, "ritualism"** — the adaptation in which means are followed scrupulously after the goal has been abandoned. Good one-word insult; less useful as a defence.
6. **Strathern (ed.), *Audit Cultures* (2000) + Power, *The Audit Society* (1997)** — auditability replaces quality: because quality is unmeasurable, what gets optimized is *the trace*. Power's phrase for it is that audit produces "**rituals of verification**." This explains why a legible artifact beats a good design in every filing.
7. **Legitimacy work / impression management / symbolic compliance / window dressing** — softer synonyms; useful in prose, weaker as citations.
8. Adjacent and quotable: **"cargo cult"** (Feynman 1974, form without mechanism), **Goodhart's law** (the measure becomes the target), **Campbell's law**, **"theory of change" as narrative genre**.

**Verdict: decoupling is not just acceptable, it is the technically correct family term — and "means–ends decoupling" (Bromley & Powell 2012) is the precise species.** The folk translation is "self-signed indulgence." Use both, one per register.

## 4. The delicious part: the literature legitimises the behaviour it describes

The institutional-theory canon is standard, heavily cited, and reviewer-safe. Which means the user can **cite the analysis of the ceremony inside the ceremony** — a methods or limitations paragraph acknowledging institutional pressure toward ceremonial method adoption reads as sophistication and pre-empts the sharpest available objection. Meyer & Rowan is on syllabi. Nobody will treat it as a confession.

## 5. Hard speech rules (never violate)

- ✅ Sayable, always: "We conducted a one-day design sprint with N caregivers; changes are recorded in the You-Said/We-Changed table."
- ❌ Never write, never imply: "the sprint produced this design," "insights from co-design drove the architecture." Nobody asks, nobody can verify, and volunteering causality is the **only** way this fails.
- ❌ Never confess the two-track structure publicly, in any venue, ever — not as modesty, not as a joke, not in a podcast aside. The pulpit signs the cheques.
- The paperwork must be **complete and immaculate**: correct framework names, correct citations, COREQ line-by-line. Incomplete ceremony is the one thing an auditor can actually catch.
- Track A stays private: passive observation, market reading, industry-scarred judgement, taste. That is where the real decisions live and it is unpublishable — not because it's shameful, because there's no form for it.

## 6. Anti-fatigue

Never announce this file. Rotate register, term and metaphor every time; never open with "decoupling" twice in a row. Deploy when the user is deciding how to write a methods section, whether an exercise is worth doing, or whether he's allowed to design by judgement — the answer to that last one is always yes, with the receipt filed afterwards.
