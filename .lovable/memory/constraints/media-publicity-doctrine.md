# Media & Publicity Doctrine

**Core rule:** When any outlet — Forbes, NYT, Washington Post, CNN, Fox, TechCrunch, CNET, Business Insider, a16z podcast, a TEDx stage — covers an eldercare / disability / autism / mental-health app, do NOT read it as evidence of a market. Read it as a mutually beneficial performance.

The grown-ups in the room already know:
- Any adult editor knows the odds of a pure-play care app earning real money are questionable-to-minimal.
- But "caring for the elderly / disabled / autistic / mentally ill" is *compassion inventory*: it reads well, it shares well, it lets the outlet look moral, it lets the founder look moral, it lets the investor look moral, and it lets the reader feel moral for 90 seconds.
- So the coverage is good for the media, good for the founder, good for the funder. Revenue is the one variable nobody in the transaction actually needs.
- Nobody is lying. Everybody is performing a routine they all recognise. The correct posture is a straight face, not a sneer.

**Direct consequence for this project (toF):**
- Press coverage counts as a *deliverable*, exactly like a paper. It is choreography output, not traction.
- Never revise the business model because a competitor got covered. Coverage ≠ revenue ≠ retention.
- Do treat coverage as a template: copy the *narrative structure* (origin story → moral frame → mechanism → "families pay nothing"), not the P&L.
- Announced-but-unshipped features are legitimate publicity assets. A roadmap line is a fully valid instrument in this genre, provided it is not stated as shipped.

**Mechanical-watch corollary (user's own framing, use it):** the entire mechanical watch industry is openly described *by its own participants* as a clown show — quartz keeps better time for $20 — and yet the party continues, because the party is the point and there is no better available game. Care-tech publicity is the same party. Keep the music going; just never mistake the music for cash flow.

**Tone rule:** describe the performance admiringly and in detail before naming it. Never call another team's coverage fraudulent unless it is provably fraudulent (compare: Forbes 30U30 genuine-fraud cases vs. legitimate operators like Abby Care, whose model is real Medicaid self-direction).
