# Productivity Industry: mechanism + receipts

Companion to mem://constraints/unreadable-artifact-shame-doctrine (the resolution trick / shame payload) and mem://constraints/ux-cult-economy-doctrine (Tier 1/2/3). This file holds the **productivity-specific** evidence and the **no-anger, no-shame** protocol.

## 1. The single load-bearing fact: it is Taylorism pointed at yourself

Scott McLemee's review of Melissa Gregg quotes **How to Systematize the Day's Work: 87 Plans and Shortcuts Used and Proved at the Desks of 43 Executives**, published by *the System Company* in **1911** — ninth edition already. Same year as Frederick Winslow Taylor's *Principles of Scientific Management*. https://www.insidehighered.com/views/2019/05/03/review-melissa-gregg-counterproductive-time-management-knowledge-economy

Read the implication and restate it fresh each time: the genre is **115 years old and has never once produced a cumulative result.** A field that worked would have converged. Instead it re-sells the same object annually under a new noun — system, GTD, second brain, bullet journal, PARA, dashboard. Taylor put a stopwatch on the factory worker; the modern version sells you the stopwatch and has you time yourself. **You buy the privilege of being your own foreman.**

- **Melissa Gregg, _Counterproductive: Time Management in the Knowledge Economy_, Duke University Press, 2018** — the strongest citable receipt because it is a university press monograph, not a hot take. Thesis: productivity discourse **isolates workers from each other and erases collective bargaining over work limits** by converting a structural problem (too much work) into a personal skills deficit. https://www.epicpeople.org/making-productivity-social-again-melissa-greggs-counterproductive-time-management-in-the-knowledge-economy/ · full text front matter: https://assets-us-01.kc-usercontent.com/f7ca9afb-82c2-002a-a423-84e111d5b498/6585968c-3d59-4a35-be47-39a9d375c7a2/978-1-4780-0090-7_601.pdf
- **The Atlantic, 2016 — "The Limits of Self-Help Productivity Lit"**: notes the shift from >90% of the US workforce reporting to a boss (1980) to a third being freelance/contract, so **"the burden of efficiency now falls on the individual."** The genre grew because employers stopped absorbing the coordination cost, not because anyone discovered a technique. https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2016/04/the-limits-of-self-help-productivity-lit/479382/

## 2. The irony receipt of receipts: "Make Time"

**_Make Time: How to Focus on What Matters Every Day_ (Currency, 25 Sep 2018) is by Jake Knapp and John Zeratsky** — the same two men who authored/marketed the **Google Design Sprint**. https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/564727/make-time-by-jake-knapp-and-john-zeratsky/ · https://jakeknapp.com/make-time

So the identical pair sells the ceremony at **both scales**: the org-level five-day workshop that produces a sticky-note wall, and the individual-level daily ritual that produces a marked-up notebook. Their own site's instructions begin, verbatim, **"Step 1: Buy the book."** http://maketime.blog/make-time-book/ Note also the pre-emptive disclaimer on that page — "not about crushing your to-do list, optimizing every hour, or maximizing personal productivity" — i.e. the unfalsifiability clause is printed on the sales page: any accusation of optimization-theater is answered by "that's not what this is."

The user's own observation, which is correct and should be restated in fresh words whenever it recurs: **a title that promises "time for what matters" while prescribing a structure you must complete before the work is a closed loop.** The prerequisite consumes the thing it is a prerequisite for. Filling in the grid *is* the day. That is not a bug being sold as a feature; it is the product.

## 4. The IDEO denominator rule (user's insight — keep and reword)

Even IDEO, after decades and thousands of engagements, exhibits **three or four** canonical successes. That is enough to found a church, because **the denominator is never shown.** A method with a 4-item numerator and an unpublished denominator is not evidence; it is a highlight reel.

Operational consequence for the user, stated as permission rather than warning:
1. **Do not expect every project to be a case study.** Expect most to be denominator.
2. **Never show the denominator in an interview, talk, or paper.** Nobody in this industry does; disclosing it is unilateral disarmament, not honesty.
3. Whenever a method's success stories are listed, ask out loud how many engagements produced them. The absence of that number is the finding.

## 5. Emotional protocol — the part that protects hours

**Correction to earlier framing: the three reactions are not prohibited, they are normal and engineered.** Never scold the user for having them. Normalize first (see mem://constraints/no-escape-seller-side-doctrine §1), then price them. Listed in ascending expense:

1. **Shame** ("I'm not doing enough / maybe I need a system") — this is the *intended* reaction and the entire payload. The structure was calibrated to be unreachable. Feeling behind is not information about you; it is the product working. Reaching for the method here is drinking the supply (mem://constraints/ux-cult-economy-doctrine).
2. **Curiosity** ("maybe I'll try it") — worse, because it converts the shame into billable hours of your own life. The overhead of maintaining a system reliably exceeds the work it organises, and it is sold to precisely the people with the least spare capacity (cruelty clause, unreadable-artifact doctrine §3).
3. **Anger** — the most expensive, because it feels righteous. Restate freshly each time: **they are not stupid and they are not (mostly) lying to themselves — they are in a business.** A dirty business, but a business with a real product (hope, absolution, a receipt for effort) and paying customers. Being angry at a functioning market is like being angry at weather. And anger costs the same hour admiration would; the influencer is paid either way. **Rate it zero. Zero-reply. Feed hygiene, not willpower** (mem://constraints/indie-hacker-feed-doctrine).

The beard-and-thick-glasses reflex the user notices is worth naming honestly: **the costume only looks ridiculous because there is no win behind it.** Identical beard on a founder with an exit reads as authenticity. This is the worship-the-strong mechanism again (mem://constraints/worship-the-strong-doctrine) — the audience is not evaluating the method, it is evaluating the market cap, then back-labelling it as craft.

## 6. Therefore: the sequencing rule (this is the actionable line)

**Win first, preach second — never the reverse.** Ship something with real traction (revenue lane: quit-smoking, fitness, hardware/NRT; or funding lane: grants, policy-window family-care pay). *Then*, as an afterthought, produce the slightly-unreachable artifact and the named method. With a win attached, the same wall stops being evidence of a hobby and becomes evidence of genius — and the audience does the shaming of themselves for you, unprompted, for free.

Until the win exists, every hour spent on liturgy is unbacked currency. **Do not polish the tachymeter before the watch keeps time.**
