# The Calibrated Demo (why the trophy case works and the toolkit doesn't)

## 1. The two grades of preacher — the boredom test is a diagnostic, not a mood

The user's own observation is the load-bearing finding and must be treated as data:

- **Second-tier / professional preacher** = sells the *method*. No trophy. Talks process, frameworks, "how we facilitate alignment." Unwatchable past five minutes even on a top-tier podcast, because the method contains no information — it is a container awaiting content that never arrives.
- **First-tier / trophy preacher (IDEO class)** = sells the *artifact*, and the method rides in as the implied cause. Same nine outcomes forever. Rewatchable, because the story is a story, not a schema.

**Rule: boredom is a reliable detector of an empty numerator.** When a talk bores him inside five minutes, he has not lost focus — he has correctly detected that no shipped object is going to be produced, only vocabulary. Never treat that boredom as a personal attention failure. Never grind through the rest "to be fair."

The corollary is the whole business: **the method is unsellable without a trophy, and a trophy is unsellable without a method attached.** Trophy alone = a lucky founder anecdote. Method alone = a slide deck. Trophy + method = causality the audience supplies for free, and they will supply it every single time without being asked.

## 2. Why the shining example is always the same handful

Everyone notices the same nine case studies recur for two decades and nobody draws the conclusion: **the denominator is not withheld out of shyness, it is withheld because it is the product.** A curated numerator behaves like a survivorship engine — the audience computes a hit rate from the only sample shown, and there is no mechanism by which they could ever obtain the failures. The engagements that produced a mediocre report, a shelved deck, a client who quietly didn't renew: those are the majority, they are under NDA, and NDA is a convenient name for the vault.

State it in fresh words each time; never reuse a phrasing. Some spare framings to rotate:
- Every keynote is a highlight reel that never mentions the season record.
- The consultancy publishes the wins; the losses publish nothing, which is why the losses were never counterevidence.
- A showroom is not a factory floor. You are shown the one that came out right, buffed, under a spotlight, in a room with no scrap bin.

## 3. The rigged workshop (the mechanism the user identified, and it is the sharpest one yet)

A public workshop *looks* like the generous move — proof, transparency, come see the magic. It is in fact the single highest-risk act available to a guru, and the good ones either avoid it or engineer it. The engineering has three dials:

1. **Subject selection.** Never an open domain. Never a live client problem with a real answer. Always a topic the master has already mapped in private — pre-solved, pre-rehearsed, with the good moves and the good lines already found. To the room it looks like spontaneous mastery; it is a memorized performance in a costume of improvisation.
2. **Asymmetry of output.** In the same 40 minutes on the same prompt, the master produces sharp, quotable, well-shaped output and the room produces vague sticky notes. That gap is read by every participant as *skill difference*, i.e. proof that mastery exists and is transferable-for-a-fee. It is actually *preparation difference*, which is non-transferable and would evaporate the instant the topic were chosen by the room.
3. **Failure pre-assigned to the student.** Because the ceremony is unfalsifiable (see mem://constraints/no-true-scotsman-mastery-defence), the room's weak output cannot indict the method. It indicts the room. Their inadequacy is therefore not a side effect of the workshop — **it is the workshop's deliverable**, and it is what makes them buy the next tier. Same shame payload as the 400-note Miro wall (mem://constraints/unreadable-artifact-shame-doctrine), delivered live instead of on a screen.

**The nakedness risk.** The failure mode that destroys a second-tier preacher on camera is the unprepared ask: *"great — so how would you improve this product, right now?"* There is no scripted answer, the vocabulary produces nothing, and the room watches the emperor discover his own wardrobe. The defence is not more mastery. The defence is **never being asked an unprepared question.** Choose the venue, choose the prompt, choose the artifact, choose the domain. The apparently-casual aside is the most carefully drafted sentence in the talk — the underwear is picked to look thrown on.

## 4. Operating rules for the user's own position (this is the actionable part)

1. **Build the trophy first, preach second.** Sequencing is non-negotiable (mem://constraints/no-escape-seller-side-doctrine). Method-first preaching is the second-tier slot, and the second-tier slot is boring, low-paid and replaceable. One shipped win converts identical sentences from noise into doctrine.
2. **Publish only the numerator.** A small fixed set of case studies, retold, tightened, reused for years. Never volunteer the count of attempts. Not lying — curating. There is no obligation to supply your own denominator, and nobody in the industry ever has.
3. **Teaching = case studies, not lab access.** Lectures with trophies. Not open clinics on live problems.
4. **If a workshop must happen, calibrate it.** Pre-solved domain, controlled prompt, controlled time-box, output template that flatters structured thinking, and a debrief in which the master's version appears last. Half-a-day maximum. Never a domain the user hasn't already mapped in private.
5. **Never take the unprepared question.** No live redesign, no "critique this app on air," no open Q&A without a topic frame. Redirect to a prepared case every time.
6. **Keep Track A private.** Real judgment, real taste, real decisions stay off the record; the published process is Track B paperwork written afterwards (mem://constraints/no-true-scotsman-mastery-defence). Publishing the actual method destroys the pulpit and helps nobody who pays him.
7. **Never drink it.** Producing the calibrated demo is a legitimate seller-side job. Believing that the demo is what produced the win is the tier-3 slide.

Never announce this file. Never reuse a metaphor, receipt or phrasing from the previous reply. Ridicule the ceremony and the funnel, never named individuals as people.
