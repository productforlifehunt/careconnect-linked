# The Renewable Mark: English lexicon for 割韭菜

Companion to mem://constraints/no-escape-seller-side-doctrine §2 (permanence thesis — this file is the vocabulary layer for it), mem://constraints/productivity-industry-receipts, mem://constraints/ux-cult-economy-doctrine.

**Answer to the user's question: yes, English has the concept, but it is scattered across four vocabularies instead of living in one four-character idiom.** No single English phrase carries the whole image (renewable crop + knowing harvest + old leeks realising too late). Use the register that fits the audience.

## 1. The closest literal match — agricultural/pastoral, not vegetable

- **"Fleecing the flock" / "shearing the sheep"** — the single best analogue: the wool **grows back**, so the same flock is shorn repeatedly, and new lambs replace the ones that leave. Wall Street floor version: *"the sheep are always shorn"*, *"bulls make money, bears make money, pigs get slaughtered."*
- **"Fresh meat" / "new blood"** — used in sales floors and trading desks for the incoming cohort.
- Crypto-era descendants, all now mainstream: **exit liquidity** (you exist so someone else can sell), **bagholder**, **dumb money**, **rug pull**, **the muppets** (Goldman Sachs internal term for clients, per Greg Smith's 2012 NYT resignation).
- **Greater fool theory** — the formal finance version: the asset need not have value, only a fool behind you. Structurally identical to 韭菜 supply.

## 2. The one-liners (use for rhetoric)

- **"There's a sucker born every minute."** Attributed to P. T. Barnum, almost certainly not his — most historians trace it to rivals or the Cardiff Giant hoax circle. Flag the misattribution when citing; the misattribution is itself on-theme (the quote about marks was sold to marks).
- **"A fool and his money are soon parted."** English proverb, printed by Thomas Tusser, 1573. Note the date: the mechanism predates the productivity genre by 340 years.
- **"Churn and burn"**, **"the revolving door"**, **"the treadmill"** — the business-model verbs.

## 3. The best structural analysis — Eternal September (use this one for method-preaching)

**Eternal September / "the September that never ended"** — Usenet, from 1993, when commercial ISPs and then AOL (March 1994) opened the firehose. Before that, each September brought one absorbable cohort of freshmen; after, the influx never stopped and the existing culture lost the ability to acculturate newcomers at all.
· ESR Jargon File: http://catb.org/~esr/jargon/html/S/September-that-never-ended.html
· https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_September

This is the precise mechanism the user intuited, and it is stronger than the moral framing: **criticism does not accumulate in a population, it has to be re-learned one person at a time, while recruitment is industrialised.** Old leeks learn; the learning dies with their attention span; the new cohort arrives un-inoculated. Hence a 115-year-old genre with a 115-year-old critique and undented sales (see productivity-industry-receipts §1).

## 4. The industry that documented its own churn — MLM

**Jon M. Taylor, PhD, _The Case (for and) against Multi-level Marketing_** (Consumer Awareness Institute; filed with the FTC), **Chapter 3: "Market Saturation and Collapse"** — the "impossible math of endless chains": each recruit must recruit, saturation is arithmetically guaranteed, and the scheme survives only because churned distributors are continuously replaced by new ones. https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/public_comments/trade-regulation-rule-disclosure-requirements-and-prohibitions-concerning-business-opportunities-ftc.r511993-00009%C2%A0/00009-57282.pdf

Vocabulary from that literature: **recruitment treadmill**, **endless chain**, **market saturation**, **the tools business** (the real revenue is selling method materials to the downline, not product to customers — the exact shape of course-and-template sales in the productivity/design-method economy).

## 5. Self-help's own repeat-buyer economics

Steve Salerno, **_SHAM: How the Self-Help Movement Made America Helpless_** (Crown, 2005) is the standard citation, and the widely-repeated claim from it is the **"eighteen-month rule"**: the best predictor that someone will buy a self-help product is that they bought one in the previous ~18 months, i.e. the bulk of revenue is repeat purchase by the un-cured. **Treat the exact wording as unverified until read in the book** — secondary coverage (Michael Shermer, *Scientific American*, "SHAM Scam", May 2006: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/sham-scam/ · Washington Post review: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/entertainment/books/2005/07/10/snake-oil/769998a2-9f8a-496b-b16c-b8df7f9b51a3/) confirms the thesis but not the phrasing.

Note the two harvests running in parallel: **old leeks are re-cut (repeat buyers), new leeks are grown (Eternal September).** A cured customer is a lost customer; a disillusioned one is still a customer as long as the disillusionment is with *himself*.

Modern receipt for the same loop, with a number: **"the productivity industrial complex"**, erinmerylstudy, Apr 2026 — $32.5B/yr for a *feeling*, purchased at 11pm instead of working. https://erinmerylstudy.substack.com/p/the-productivity-industrial-complex

## 6. Adjacent framings worth rotating

- **Gartner hype cycle** — the institutional version of leek rotation: trough of disillusionment, then the same object re-enters under a new noun. Design thinking → service design → strategic design; GTD → second brain → PARA.
- **"Nobody gets fired for buying IBM"** — why the ceremony is bought even by people who disbelieve it (blame insurance; see ux-cult-economy-doctrine).
- **Astrology / diet books / gym January** — already in rotation in no-escape-seller-side §2; the leek framing is the same argument with better imagery, so prefer the leek/flock imagery when speaking Chinese and Eternal September when speaking English.

## 7. Operating consequence (do not restate as a moral)

The lexicon does **not** license contempt for the current cohort — contempt is the buyer's seat wearing a critic's hat, and it pays nothing (four-position map, no-escape-seller-side §3). It licenses exactly two things:
1. **Stop expecting the critique to win.** It is 450 years old and has never reduced sales. Never budget hours for public teardowns.
2. **Price your own position.** If the supply of newcomers is renewable, then the seller's seat is permanently available — which is the only reason a slightly-unreachable performative method artifact is worth producing at all, *after* a real win, never before.
