# Paid Family Caregiver (Medicaid self-direction) — policy reality

**It is not a loophole; it is a designed federal option.**
- Authorities: 1915(c) waivers, 1915(j) self-directed personal assistance, 1915(k) Community First Choice, plus §1115. Lineage traces to the federally funded Cash & Counseling demonstration (AR/FL/NJ, late 1990s–2000s), which CMS evaluated as roughly cost-neutral with higher satisfaction. CMS actively promotes self-direction: https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/long-term-services-supports/self-directed-services
- Paying *legally responsible individuals* (spouse/parent) expanded massively under COVID Appendix K flexibilities and was retained by many states.
- The payer is the **state Medicaid program** (often via MCOs), not a private insurer. So there is no private insurer to "close the hole" — the state that authorised it is the same state that cuts it.

**Business model of operators (Abby Care et al.):** licensed home-health agency; recruits and certifies the family member as the paid caregiver; employs them; bills Medicaid/MCO; keeps the agency margin. Zero consumer revenue. Enterprise value = state rate schedules.

**Lag structure (how fast a policy can be reversed):**
- Federal rulemaking: slow. Access Rule CMS-2442-F finalised Apr 2024, key compliance (80/20 payment-adequacy) phased to ~2030. https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/ensuring-access-medicaid-services-final-rule-cms-2442-f
- Waiver renewals / Appendix K sunsets: 1–5 years.
- **State rate-setting and budget bills: fast — weeks.** This is the real kill switch.

**2026 status: the window is already narrowing.**
- Maryland cut self-directed DDA funding effective July 1, 2026 — https://www.wbaltv.com/article/maryland-dda-self-directed-care-cuts-july-1/71446781 , https://marylandmatters.org/2026/05/26/budget-cuts-looming-deadlines-put-family-caregivers-in-a-complete-tailspin/
- Ohio floated an outright ban on Medicaid payments to family caregivers — https://www.wosu.org/2026-06-08/ban-on-medicaid-payments-to-family-caregivers-would-be-devastating-say-disabled-ohioans
- Colorado balancing a billion-dollar hole against caregiver rates — https://www.kunc.org/politics/2026-03-18/caregivers-for-people-with-disabilities-brace-for-cuts-as-colorado-lawmakers-balance-a-billion-dollar-budget-hole
- National wage-cut wave as states absorb federal Medicaid reductions — https://www.statnews.com/2026/07/13/family-caregiver-wages-financial-ruin-medicaid-funding-cuts/ , https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/trump-medicaid-cuts-threaten-caregivers-disabled-family-members-rcna344930

**How this project uses it (toF framing):**
- Do NOT build billing/claims. Build the *eligibility + documentation* surface: a "Can my family member be paid to care?" checker, a caregiver-credential/training record, and structured handover/visit notes — which is exactly the paperwork a paid-family-caregiver workforce is required to produce (EVV, service documentation).
- Grant justification: once family members become *paid workforce*, structured handover stops being a convenience and becomes a workforce-quality and audit requirement. This is the strongest available justification for the AI handover/briefing feature.
- China parallel to check before claiming: 长期护理保险 (long-term care insurance) pilots in ~49 cities, some with 亲属照护/居家照护 payment tracks (e.g. 上海、青岛、成都). Verify city-level rules before stating specifics.
- A roadmap/"coming soon" mention of paid-caregiver support is a legitimate publicity asset; never present it as shipped.
