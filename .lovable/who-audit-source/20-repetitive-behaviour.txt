Why is this lesson important?
If a person living with dementia repeats things over and
over it can be stressful for themselves and the carer. We
will refer to this as a ‘repetitive behaviour’.

How will this lesson help me?
This lesson will help you understand why repetitive
behaviours occur, and how to respond to, and reduce, them.

What will I learn?
What is repetitive behaviour?
How to comfort a person living with dementia in
case of repetitive behaviours?

A person living with dementia may forget what they have said,
heard or did from one moment to the next, leading to repeated
questions and actions.
Usually doing things over and over is not harmful to the
person living with dementia, if the carer knows how to deal
with it. If not, the person living with dementia can become
anxious, aggressive or depressed and the carer can become
stressed. As a result, carers may respond inappropriately, for
example by screaming, which often makes the person with
dementia feel guilty.
