Why is this lesson important?

1. How may your roles change over time?

Dealing with dementia is a journey you will take together,
because it changes the daily life of both the person
living with dementia and the carer.

You may feel as if your role is changing. In the early stages
of dementia, you may still be able to enjoy many different
activities together.

How will this lesson help me?

As dementia progresses, the person will have more difficulty
with everyday tasks. This will require a carer to do more to
help with everyday life, such as giving medications and taking
the person with dementia to appointments.

This lesson teaches you skills for your journey together:
to stay connected, involve others, take care of yourself,
and plan for the future.

2. How to stay connected with the person with dementia?

Tip

It is important to keep talking together. The sooner you start
talking about dementia and its effects on you and the person
you care for, the better. Dementia will make doing this more
difficult as time goes by. Talk about the changes happening
now and what might happen in the future. Stay connected with
each other and discuss the changes that may occur over time.

Here are some small tips to help you communicate better
with someone living with dementia:
zz use simple sentences;
zz try to stay in control of your feelings;
zz create time for yourself and for each other.

For more tips on how to improve communication with the person
living with dementia, including in the later stages of the disease,

3. How to plan pleasant activities and relaxation?
Providing care may become increasingly time consuming as
the disease progresses. Sometimes carers do not take enough
time for themselves. It is really important to try and take care of
yourself as best as you can, as some carers may break down from
the stress. Part of the journey together should include planning
pleasant activities and relaxation for yourself.

4. How to involve others?
Caregiving can sometimes challenge friendships and relationships.
It is common for people providing care to feel that no one
understands what they are going through. Talking about your
thoughts and feelings with others might help make you feel better.

Caring for someone with dementia does not have to be a lonely
experience. Let friends and family members know when you could
use a break. Caring for a loved one with dementia is sometimes too
big a job for one person.

5. How to plan well for the future?
Preparing for the future is a topic that will need to be
discussed. You will need to make decisions with the person
you care for about preferences for who might provide care,
planning for the costs of future care needs, and preferences in
case of more advanced care needs and end of life decisions.

Many people providing care, like you, worry about the future.
The earlier you plan for the future, the better and easier it will
be for you. Planning early will also ensure that the person you
care for will be better able to say what their wishes are. Where
possible, discuss topics that concern you.

Intimate relationships may change

Let’s revisit the scenario with Olivia and Jacob

As you progress through the journey together, partners may
experience some changes in their intimate relationship.

Olivia is in the early stages of dementia and lives with her
husband Jacob. Olivia and Jacob have a healthy, mutually
satisfying physical relationship, but Jacob is worried about the
day when these relations may stop.

At first, sexual relationships between partners may still be
possible. As the disease progresses, sexual relationships and
physical intimacy may become more difficult.

Here are some tips for partners on how to maintain intimacy,
even when sexual relations may no longer be possible with the
person living with dementia.

Tip
Ways to stay connected
zz Use touch, hold hands or do a gentle massage to
communicate warmth, connection, safety and love.
zz Use music – it is truly a universal language. Play
music that the person you care for likes and that
makes them feel good.

iSupport
Intervention
Manual

zz Try to maintain your sense of humour. This will help
reduce frustration and tension.
zz Talk with them about what they like and need – it’s
an important conversation to have more than once,
as things change over time.

Taking care of yourself
One of the most important things to remember as roles
change is that you, as someone providing care for a person
with dementia, need to care for yourself too. Throughout this
manual we will teach you ways to do this.

Reminder
zz It is okay to take a break.
zz It is okay to ask for help.
zz It is okay to feel frustrated sometimes.
zz It is okay to feel lonely sometimes if you feel that the
person you care for is no longer able to meaningfully
communicate with you.
